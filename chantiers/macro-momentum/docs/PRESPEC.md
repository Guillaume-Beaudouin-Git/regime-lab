# Pre-specification — frozen before any result was computed

The independent practitioner scan that motivated this study found that the
data-mined link in every regime-conditioned strategy is **not the signal but the
map from signal to position**. Reproducing a published index methodology and
comparing it against 160,000 random factor combinations placed the official one
at the 98th percentile — a score obtained by trying many, not by understanding.

So the map is written here first, from textbook macroeconomic reasoning, and it
is never fitted. Every sign below can be argued from a first-year macro course,
which is the point: if a sign needs a backtest to justify it, it is a parameter.

## The four themes

Each is a **change**, never a level. The distinction is the study's whole
premise: a companion project established that the *state* of the economy carries
information about variance and none about direction, while practitioners who
trade macro directionally trade its *rate of change*.

| theme | measured as |
| --- | --- |
| Growth | one-year change in the year-on-year growth of industrial production and payrolls, averaged |
| Inflation | one-year change in year-on-year CPI |
| Policy | one-year change in the three-month bill rate |
| Risk | one-year change in the Baa-Treasury credit spread |

## The sign map

Read as: when the theme **rises**, hold this sign in this asset class.

| theme rising | equities | bonds | commodities | US dollar |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Growth | **+1** | **−1** | **+1** | **+1** |
| Inflation | **−1** | **−1** | **+1** | **−1** |
| Policy tightening | **−1** | **−1** | **−1** | **+1** |
| Credit spread | **−1** | **+1** | **−1** | **+1** |

Reasoning, one line each, so a reader can disagree with a specific cell rather
than with the table as a whole:

* **Growth**: expanding output raises earnings and commodity demand, lifts real
  rates against bonds, and attracts capital to the currency.
* **Inflation**: erodes nominal bond coupons and equity multiples through the
  discount rate, raises commodity prices directly, and weakens the currency's
  purchasing power.
* **Policy**: a rising short rate lifts the discount rate against both equities
  and bonds, raises the cost of carrying commodities, and widens the rate
  differential in the currency's favour.
* **Credit**: a widening spread is the market pricing default risk — equities and
  commodities fall, government bonds are bid, and the dollar receives the
  safe-haven flow.

Two cells are genuinely contestable and are flagged rather than hidden. Growth
against the dollar is ambiguous in the literature (the risk-on channel pushes
the other way), and policy against commodities depends on whether the tightening
is a response to inflation. **Both are left as written and reported as-is**;
changing them after seeing results would be the exact failure this file exists
to prevent.

## What a result has to beat

* **Placebo on the map**: the same signals with sign maps drawn at random.
  Reported as the percentile the real map reaches in that distribution. This is
  the test the published index methodology failed.
* **Placebo on the timing**: the same map with the signal replaced by a
  duty-cycle-matched random series.
* **Unconditional benchmark**: buy and hold each asset class, volatility
  targeted, since a long-biased signal would otherwise look like an edge.
* **Costs**: charged at the levels declared in the code before any result.
