# Hypothesis 2 — falsified, and the raw material is measurably gone

Cross-country macro momentum on market-based state variables, nineteen
sovereigns, monthly, 1990 to 2026. Nine theme-by-asset-class cells tested
separately **before** any book was assembled, which is the discipline hypothesis
1 lacked.

## The nine cells

| theme | bonds | currencies | equities |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| policy | IC −0.023, t −1.13 | IC −0.016, t −0.68 | IC −0.017, **t −1.45** |
| slope | IC −0.009, t −0.38 | IC +0.023, t +0.95 | IC +0.001, t +0.10 |
| currency | IC +0.028, t +0.80 | IC −0.013, t −0.56 | IC −0.010, t −0.36 |

**Zero of nine cells reaches |t| = 1.96, where chance gives 0.5.** The largest
statistic in the table is 1.45. The Šidák threshold for nine tests is 2.77 and
nothing comes close.

No book was built. There is nothing to build one from.

## Why: the cross-section was abolished by monetary union

A relative strategy needs countries to differ. They have stopped.

| period | dispersion of short rates | of long rates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1990-1998 | **2.30%** | 1.72% |
| 1999-2007 | 1.37% | 1.01% |
| 2008-2015 | 1.08% | 1.44% |
| 2016-2026 | **0.89%** | 0.91% |

And among the nine sovereigns that adopted the euro, the standard deviation of
their own short rates:

```
1990-1998 : 2.35%
1999-2007 : 0.00%
2016-2026 : 0.00%
```

**Exactly zero.** Nine of the nineteen sovereigns became one policy instrument
overnight in 1999. The panel reads nineteen names and carries, on the policy
theme, eleven independent ones — and on the currency theme, ten.

## The split, and what it classifies as

| period | cells above \|t\| 1.96 | largest \|t\| |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before the euro, 1990-1998 | 0 of 4 | 1.74 (policy → bonds) |
| after the euro, 1999-2026 | 0 of 9 | 0.95 (slope → currencies) |

Two different kills, and they need separate labels.

**After 1999 it is mechanical.** The dispersion that the strategy trades is
measurably zero for nine of nineteen sovereigns. No estimator recovers a
cross-section that does not exist.

**Before 1999 it is a power failure, not a rejection.** Nine years, four usable
cells, a largest statistic of 1.74 — that is an underpowered sample, and under
the reopening policy an underpowered result is reopenable while a mechanical one
is not.

## What this says about the published result it was modelled on

AQR report a Sharpe of 1.2 for macro momentum over **1970-2016**. That sample is
dominated by the era this study can only see nine years of: independent national
monetary policies, inflation rates that differed by whole percentage points
between neighbours, and currencies that moved on their own.

**The strategy did not decay. Its raw material was legislated away.** Running it
today on developed markets means ranking countries that share a central bank.

The dispersion still exists in emerging markets — and so does the reason nobody
tests this for free: their vintaged macro is not public, and their currencies
carry a capital-controls risk that no backtest on spot prices captures.

## Classification

* **Post-1999 developed markets: mechanical kill, closed.**
* **Pre-1999, and emerging markets: power failure, reopenable** — but only with a
  sample that actually has cross-sectional dispersion, which means paid data.
